# Inventory structure

Follow this exactly. Every number is labeled by source: published list price, company-reported, or estimated.

```
# Tech Stack Inventory: [Company Name]

**Prepared:** [date]
**Company:** [headcount] people, [vertical]
**Source of this inventory:** [from memory / finance export / admin console]

---

## Data caveat

[How complete this is and why. If built from memory, say plainly that shadow tools
are almost certainly missing and that finance export is the fix. Name which prices
are published list versus reported versus estimated. Omit nothing uncomfortable.]

---

## What you run

| Tool | Function | Who owns it | Cost basis | AI in it today |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|----------------|

Cost basis is one of: published list, reported by you, estimated. Never blend them.
AI in it today is one of: included, paid add-on, none, unknown.

---

## AI you are already paying for

[Tools on the list that shipped AI features they may not have turned on. Cheapest
win available. For each: what it does, whether it is included or an add-on, and
what it would take to switch on.]

---

## Overlap

[Two tools doing one job. Name both, say which the evidence favors and why, and
name what would have to be true for the other to win. Do not recommend
consolidation without naming the migration cost.]

---

## Where AI would actually help

[Tied to the pain named in the interview, not to a generic list. Each item: the
task, who does it, roughly how often, and the specific tool or capability that
addresses it.]

---

## Where AI does not belong

[At least one. Name a place in their stack where adding AI would be a bad trade,
and why. A document that only ever says yes is a sales deck.]

---

## What to do next

[Three items maximum, ordered. Each specific enough to assign to a person with a
date. Not "evaluate AI options".]

---

*Created by Blane Warrene, blanewarrene.com*
```
