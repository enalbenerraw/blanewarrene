# Course structure

Sized to the real starting point. Three good sessions beat eight abandoned ones.

```
# AI Enablement Course: [Company Name]

**Prepared:** [date]
**For:** [who is being trained]
**Starting point:** [never used / informal use / already using and doing it badly]
**Format:** [live / recorded / self-serve] · **Facilitator:** [who, or none]

---

## What this course will not fix

[Named up front. Training does not solve a tooling gap, a policy vacuum, or a
manager who does not want this. Say which of these apply here.]

---

## Session plan

| # | Session | Length | They leave having |
|---|---------|--------|-------------------|

The last column is always something they did, never something they heard.

---

## Session [n]: [title]

**Before:** [what to have ready]

**Run it**
1. [Step, with what the facilitator says]

**Exercise**
[Uses their own work, not a toy example. States what done looks like.]

**What will go wrong**
[The predictable failure in this session and the recovery.]

**If someone asks something you cannot answer**
[What to say. This is the most common facilitator failure and it deserves a
scripted answer.]

---

## How you will know it worked

[One observable measure. A specific task, a named team, a stated date. Not
attendance, not satisfaction.]

**If nothing changed at eight weeks:** the problem is usually not the course. It
is that nobody's job actually requires the new behavior. [What to check.]

---

*Created by Blane Warrene, blanewarrene.com*
```
