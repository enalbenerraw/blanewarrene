# Signal Watch brief structure

Follow this exactly. Substitute the time window you were given into the headings that carry it; do not leave a placeholder and do not default to 6 months when told otherwise.

```
# Signal Watch: [Company Name]

**Prepared:** [date]
**Company:** [Company Name] ([URL])
**Window of analysis:** [time window]

---

## Executive summary

- [Five to eight bullets. The most important findings only, conclusions not setup.]
- [If a load-bearing number is company-reported rather than verified, say so here, not only in the caveat.]

---

## Data caveat

[Carry this forward from the research dossier. Name which figures are
company-reported or self-reported rather than independently verified, and which
leadership roles are unconfirmed. Omit the heading entirely only if nothing was
unverified, which is rare for a private company.]

---

## 1. Company profile

**What they do.** [One sentence, in plain language rather than their own framing.]

**Customer segments and target market.** [SMB, mid-market, enterprise, with the industries that actually show up in their evidence.]

**Business model and pricing.** [Where public. Say "not publicly disclosed" rather than inferring a number.]

**Products and platform positioning.** [What they sell and how they frame the set.]

**Primary competitors.** [Named, with one clause each on what makes them the comparison.]

**Recent notable events.** [Funding, acquisitions, leadership changes, launches, inside the window.]

---

## 2. Executive leadership map

| Role | Name | Scope | Recent public signals |
|------|------|-------|----------------------|
| CEO | | | |
| President or COO | | | |
| CPO or Head of Product | | | |
| CMO or Head of Marketing | | | |
| CRO or Head of Sales | | | |
| CTO or Head of Engineering | | | |

Write `Unconfirmed` in the Name column where the role could not be verified. Do not leave a row out because you could not fill it; an unconfirmed CRO is itself a signal, and a reader who assumes the seat is filled will be wrong in the room.

---

## 3. Public messaging analysis

### [time window] themes
- [What they are saying, repeatedly. Three to five.]

### Stated priorities
- [What is emphasized most, and what is conspicuously de-emphasized.]

### Messaging shifts
- [Clear changes against older positioning. If none is detectable, say so.]

### Proof points
- [Campaigns, launches, partnerships, customer evidence cited repeatedly.]

### Contradictions and gaps
- [Where the claim diverges from the demonstration.]

---

## 4. Strategic synthesis

**Selling versus signaling.** [What they sell today against what the messaging signals is coming. The gap is the finding. One paragraph.]

**GTM motion.** [Product-led, sales-led, channel, enterprise, or hybrid, with the evidence.]

**Strategic priorities.**
1. **[Short label].** [One sentence of evidence and inference.]
2. ...

**What is changing.** [One paragraph.]

---

## Sources

- [Title](URL)
- ...

---

*Created by Blane Warrene, blanewarrene.com*
```
